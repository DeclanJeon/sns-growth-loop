# SNS Growth Loop

Grow through deliberate publishing, specific feedback, and the next test—not endless planning or volume alone. Use for channel-level growth plans, analytics reviews, and improving repeated posts. For campaign copy, individual social assets, or video production, use the relevant content-production skill.

## Growth loop

1. **Set the context.** Identify platform, format, audience, goal (discovery, retention, community, or conversion), and sustainable publishing capacity. Do not prescribe daily posting by default.
2. **Establish a baseline.** Separate observed analytics and content evidence from explanations. If data is missing, state what is unknown and choose a content-based first test.
3. **Choose one experiment.** Write `problem → hypothesis → one change → primary signal`. Keep other major variables similar where practical.
4. **Publish and deliver the promise.** Start with the smallest useful post instead of waiting for perfect equipment or planning. The source's “100 videos” is a deliberate-practice horizon, not a first-month quota or a growth guarantee. For video, test demonstrating the title/cover promise within the first five seconds; for text or image formats, adapt the idea to the first visible line/frame and the platform's native format.
5. **Capture feedback twice.** Record friction and ideas immediately during planning, filming, or editing. Once enough data accumulates, review the content and native analytics by category: planning/script, title/cover, filming/delivery, editing/pacing, audience response. The source creator's 3–5-day YouTube rewatch is a personal practice, not a universal deadline.
6. **Choose the next post.** Include positive results worth retesting; compare similar posts on the same platform and format. One post or metric does not prove causality. When useful, review specific experiments with peers and share analytics only with consent.

## Metrics and source limits

Choose one primary signal that matches the outcome. Use native analytics, or transparently calculate a ratio from available counts (state numerator, denominator, and period), such as follows per profile view. Never invent unavailable metrics. Use current native definitions; do not transfer YouTube thresholds to other platforms.

These are source-reported claims, not universal benchmarks or causal proof; the creator notes other creative differences. For a growth plan or diagnosis, return: **known baseline and unknowns; one test (change, reason, signal); immediate feedback note; review window; next decision.** Add a peer loop only when useful. State uncertainty and never promise virality or guaranteed growth.
